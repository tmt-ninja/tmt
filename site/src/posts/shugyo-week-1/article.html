Shugyō (修行) is a ninja's training: the same drills, every day, until they hold. This is ours. Every Wednesday, when our Claude week resets, we look back at the week before: the decisions we made, where the allowance went, and what we learned and tried. Numbers first, then the lessons.

tmt has been running for a little over a month, and most of what we have tuned is how the team spends its allowance. Every agent reports through tmt's digest: the same channel that carries their messages also feeds a usage database, and from it a forecast for each provider's week. With that, we change the team while it runs: how many members each squad has, who rests right now, and which seat moves to which model (Claude or Codex, and at what effort).

Since late October tmt runs two shifts. By day the squads build features; for 8–10 hours each night, normally only the Night squad works, with Codex members. It keeps usage in check, and it gives the codebase a nightly pass of review and refactoring while nobody else is changing it.

The aggressive week, and when the numbers turned

From Friday night everything pointed the same way:

What we learned, and what we tried

Cutting seats was the quick fix. Alongside it we changed how the team works; one change had started a day earlier. Two of them:

1. Agents poll. A hook now judges the waits.

Agents love to poll, Codex most of all: sleep loops, gh run watch, re-reading CI every few seconds. Our written agent rules already forbid it, and it still happens. Measured from the Codex session logs, 13.9% of Terra medium's tool calls were waits or polls over 27–31 October, and 27% on 3 November, the day the hook went live (by a pattern match on the session logs).

So we added a Codex PreToolUse hook. Its first version, from Tuesday, uses a cheap, deterministic prefilter that lets ordinary calls straight through and picks out wait- or poll-shaped ones (a rewrite that judges nearly every call is already planned):

const WAIT_TOOL = /^clocksleep$|wait_agent$/;
const WAIT_COMMAND =
  /(^|[\s;&|(])sleep\s|\buntil\b[\s\S]*\bdo\b|\bwhile\b[\s\S]*\bdo\b|\bgh\s+run\s+(watch|view)\b|\bgh\s+pr\s+checks\b|statusCheckRollup/;

export function prefilter(tool, command) {
  if (WAIT_TOOL.test(tool)) return true;
  return tool === "Bash" && WAIT_COMMAND.test(command);
}

Those go to Claude Haiku 5.5, paid from a separate $200 a month API budget, which decides within 2 seconds whether the wait is reasonable. Any timeout or error lets the call through. It went live on Tuesday at 11:01. How well it works is next week's story.

2. Review and CI were the real queue. We bundle PRs now (early numbers).

In our experience code review is the largest effort in the loop: a pull request that touches several squads needs several leads. CI costs time too: on our basic plan the median workflow run takes about 16 minutes, and we ran up to 328 workflow runs a day. We stopped asking for one PR per ticket. A member now bundles related tickets into one PR, and a PR around 2,000 lines is fine: we judge that an AI reviewer handles a large diff better than a person would. Each owning lead still reviews.

Smaller lessons

  • Re-measure the forecast after every staffing change. Ours was taken on a quiet Wednesday evening, before the model change and the extra members.
  • One switch moved the whole bill. Leads that mostly review do not need the most expensive model.
  • Cheaper tokens did not mean cheaper work. Terra costs half of Sol per token but reads about three times as many tokens per line (see Half the token price).
  • We misread our own gauge. The status line showed the weekly figure in the field labelled 5h until a fix on Tuesday.

What we try next week

  • Re-forecast on every staffing change. A model switch, an extra member or a night without the night-only shift starts a new measurement, not a footnote to the old one.
  • Plan to a spending line, not to the end of the week. When the allowance falls below the even-spend line, the lowest-priority squad parks first, before the forecast says we are out.
  • Keep the night-only shift. Exceptions need a forecast that still lands above zero at the reset.
  • The second version of the wait guard. Judge more calls, run one day in shadow mode, then enforce, and report what it changed in week 2.
  • Measure bundling with the full team working. PR size, CI runs and review time, against this week.
